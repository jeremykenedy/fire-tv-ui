# Backgrounds and overlays

Open **Theme and wallpaper > Menu backgrounds**. The page separates the full background from a transparent overlay. Setting the background to **Off: use my wallpaper** preserves the launcher wallpaper. The overlay is drawn above that wallpaper and below Home's cards and controls. Removing an overlay does not change the wallpaper.

Each layer has its own choice of effect, still or animated mode, speed, density and colors. The full background has a brightness control; the transparent overlay has opacity. Still mode keeps a fixed composition without a frame callback. Paused, hidden and detached layers stop their animation clock and resume without jumping forward.

All twelve existing animated effects are also available as overlays. Additional motifs are falling snow, drifting snowflakes, falling leaves, falling hearts, floating hearts, drifting petals and floating lights. These are native vector and light effects scaled to the display, with independent particle positions and motion. There is no text or watermark in a rendered scene.

## Holiday choices

**Holiday themes** offers each recipe as either a background or an overlay, animated or still. The optional coordinated icon colors retain each application's recognizable artwork. Choosing an overlay preserves the current full background; choosing a background preserves the current overlay. App order, channel selections, accounts and TV power settings are not part of a holiday preset.

The catalog includes New Year, Lunar New Year, Valentine's Day, St. Patrick's Day, Easter, Christmas, Epiphany, Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Hanukkah, Passover, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Purim, Diwali, Holi, Navratri, Janmashtami, Ganesh Chaturthi, Vaisakhi, Gurpurab, Vesak, Losar, Obon, Mahavir Jayanti, Paryushan, Naw-Ruz, Ridvan, Nowruz, Mehregan, Kwanzaa, Halloween, Thanksgiving, US Independence Day, Juneteenth, both solstices and both equinoxes.

These are decorative color and motion recipes. They do not depict sacred figures or claim that a palette is an official religious symbol. The catalog is not an exhaustive world holiday calendar. Users can combine any motif and palette and keep a named setup for another celebration. No dates or religious calendar rules are guessed or scheduled automatically.

The community names were cross-checked against the [SGPC's description of Gurpurbs](https://new.sgpc.net/gurpurbs/), the [United Nations' Vesak observance](https://www.un.org/en/observances/vesak-day), [Shrimad Rajchandra Mission's Paryushan explanation](https://www.srmd.org/wisdom/articles/what-is-paryushan), [Bahá'ís of Canada's holy days](https://www.bahai.ca/en/holy-days/), and [FEZANA's discussion of Nowruz and Mehrgan](https://fezana.org/fjissue/FEZANA_2006_03_Fall.pdf). The art and color selections are original design choices, not descriptions taken from those sources.
